\section{Conclusion}
Path-Dependent Radix Spaces provides a language-neutral systems realization of indexed finite-domain enumeration. It combines an external dependent schema, canonical intermediate representation, independent Python, C, and Rust runtimes, exact schema-relative object identities, and reproducible campaign primitives. The underlying cardinality, rank, and unrank construction belongs to established enumerative and dependent-enumeration work.

The evidence supports a precise operational claim. PDRS can count, index, reconstruct, sample, and allocate valid objects without enumerating prior objects. Coordinated rank policies eliminate duplicate assignments. A schema hash and rank support exact object reconstruction under a fixed canonicalization. These properties are useful in large finite campaigns and in the FinSpace case study.

The negative results define the boundary. Object-uniform sampling does not dominate targeted generation. Contiguous intervals can balance object counts while severely imbalancing work. Dense ranks minimize fixed-width schema-relative identity length but provide weak corruption detection. Entropy coders can beat fixed ranks under nonuniform distributions and long blocks. Arbitrary schema changes can move every rank. Execution replay requires substantially more evidence than object identity.

The resulting position is narrower and stronger than a universal testing or compression claim. PDRS offers a distinctive combination of exact indexing, cross-language execution, duplicate-free assignment, and operational reproducibility. Targeted, adaptive, interaction-covering, solver-backed, and stochastic methods remain preferable when their corresponding objectives govern the workflow.
